### What happened (Oct 13, from the ledger)
- **Entry, Oct 9.** Sold the 310 call and the 310 put, and bought the 360 call and the 260 put (exp. Oct 16),
  for a net credit of **17.60** per unit. DPZ reported Oct 13, before the open.
- **Close, 14:46 ET Oct 13.**
  - One close was sent at the natural, a **17.28** debit, inside the primary window (14:30–15:00).
  - It did not fill. It was cancelled about an hour later. **No second attempt was made before the
    close.**
  - The cause: GitHub ran the job at 14:46 and 15:46 ET, and next only after 16:00. The "every 10
    minutes" cron fires were dropped.
- **`exit_late` = false**, because it is set by the time the FIRST close was sent, not by the fill.
- **Spot by put-call parity** at the 14:46 quotes: ≈ **298.8**, down about 2.8% from the 307.4 gate spot.

### My expectations for what happened on Oct 14 (written blind)
1. **The close retried** at the first paper-options run after 09:30 ET, at that run's natural.
   - P(filled by the Oct 14 close) = **0.65**.
   - Why not higher: a limit at the natural did not fill in 15+ minutes on Oct 13. So the paper engine does
     not reliably fill a 4-leg order at the sum of the far sides, or a quote went stale (the 360 call's last
     quote was 9 minutes old).
2. **If it filled on Oct 14:**
   - P&L per unit: central **+1.0** (+$100); 80% range −2.0 to +4.0.
   - The reasoning: one more day of decay helps the short fly, but at a natural with spreads this wide most
     of it is given back.
3. **If it is still open at expiry (Fri Oct 16):** D011 settles at intrinsic on the expiry close: P&L per unit
   = 17.60 − |S − 310| while 260 ≤ S ≤ 360. At S = 299 that is **+6.6**. This is better than any natural
   close, which itself shows that holding to expiry avoids the exit spread (D010 v2 found hold-to-expiry
   did not help on average).
4. **Classification:** whatever the fill time, the frozen report counts DPZ as an on-time exit. That is a
   blind corner (Part 3).
